A focused system for a small multi‑outlet cloud kitchen

In one sentence

It keeps vendors, purchase orders, deliveries, stock, bills and payments in one place — and then tells the kitchen what to order, how much, from whom, and by when.

The problem it replaces

  • Vendor rates live in WhatsApp, orders in a notebook, bills in a drawer, stock in someone’s head.
  • Payments get missed, and vendors who are always late are never actually compared.
  • Stock runs out mid‑service, or sits until it spoils. Nobody knows which, until it happens.
  • Nobody records how much material was used — so nothing can be predicted.
The one idea that makes the AI possible
Dishes sold × recipe = raw material consumed

Usage is never typed in by hand. Sales data becomes consumption data automatically through the recipe — and consumption is the one number every prediction depends on, whether it is reorder timing, stockout risk or deadstock.

1System architecture

Four layers, one backend. Each layer only does what it is genuinely good at, and the boundary between them is the most important decision in the whole design.

Browser — React dashboard Dashboard · Vendors · Purchase Orders · Stock · Recipes · Finance · AI Insights · Assistant
▼HTTPS + login token
Backend — FastAPI (Python)one service, four internal layers
1
Core logic — plain code, no AI Stock balances, PO totals, bill due dates, outstanding amounts, order status. These must be exactly right, so a model is never allowed near them.
2
Analytics — formulas, not machine learning Daily usage rate, days of cover, real vendor lead time, delay rate, reorder point, safety stock, deadstock, overstock, wastage variance. Every figure here is explainable line by line.
3
Forecast — the only real model Fourteen‑day demand per material, per outlet. Always checked against a simple baseline; if the model cannot beat the baseline, the baseline is used and that fact is shown openly.
4
Claude — explains and answers Turns the computed numbers into a plain sentence, and answers typed questions by calling a fixed set of safe, read‑only functions. It never calculates and never writes to the database.

The rule that holds the design together

Layers 1 and 2 decide. Layer 4 only speaks. Every recommendation is produced by ordinary code, with its numbers attached to it. Claude is then handed those numbers and asked to write one sentence. That is why the advice can always be audited, and why a wrong answer traces back to a formula rather than to a model’s mood.

3Three decisions worth defending

1.  Consumption is derived from sales and recipes, never typed in — otherwise nobody records it, and the AI has nothing to work with.

2.  Stock is always the sum of its movements, never a stored number — stored quantities drift away from their own history.

3.  The model explains and retrieves; it never calculates — so the numbers stay correct and the advice stays traceable.
